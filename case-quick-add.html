Project Overview

A prior test, Quick Add from the PLP, had shipped with a conditional win. Adding to cart rose 6.0% and unique units added rose 2.4%, both significant, but product views dropped 3.9%. Everything downstream of the add, checkout started, orders completed, revenue, showed a positive but not statistically significant trend.

The read was that Quick Add accelerated early commitment but skipped the confidence-building step users normally get on the product page. The mandate that followed was to fix that at the modal itself, clearer confirmation, easier variant selection, and more control before checkout.

Key Insights

01
Engagement vs. Confidence
Quick Add moved people to act faster, but the drop in product views meant they explored less before committing.
02
Missing Confirmation
Without a clear confirmation or a way to edit the add, those incremental adds were less likely to reach checkout.
03
Isolating Variables
Shipping the redesign and the carousel together would make it impossible to tell which change drove any result.
04
Unbounded Layout
The modal had no max width, so above roughly 1920px it grew unbounded and broke visually.

The Framework

Two tests instead of one, so each change could be attributed on its own.

Test 1 · Shipped
Content & Layout Redesign
Clearer CTAs, variant selection, and confirmation. Product image kept static to isolate this change.
Test 2 · Running
Product Image Carousel
Runs on top of whichever layout won Test 1, so its effect can be measured on its own.

From a static component to a shipped, monitored test.

Status & Next Step

Conditional Ship
Checkout started rose 2.08% (just at the edge of significance), order completion rose 1.03%, and revenue per visitor rose 1.48%. No guardrail was violated, and the redesign shipped to 100% of users with monitoring. The carousel is now testing on top of it, isolated.

Reflection

The pressure to ship one big change instead of two smaller ones is real, it feels slower to isolate variables. Keeping the carousel as a separate test meant a slightly slower rollout, but it meant the next decision about whether to keep the carousel will be backed by its own data, not a guess about which part of a combined change mattered.

The responsive bug taught a similar lesson, an urgent fix and a larger redesign are easy to conflate under time pressure. Separating them into two tickets got the fix out fast without putting the bigger test at risk.
