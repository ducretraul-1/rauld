Designing an AI-assisted workflow from ad to landing page

Connecting paid, creative, design, data, and engineering around one structured brief.

Context

Marketing and design were working from different inputs, so the message users saw in an ad could drift from the experience they found on the landing page.

The challenge was not simply creating pages faster. It was creating a shared structure that connected the ad angle, audience, page format, and implementation.

The problem wasn't the landing page. It was the handoff.

Paid, creative, design, data, and engineering were contributing at different stages of the process. That created three recurring problems.

01
The ad angle could get lost between teams.
02
Design reviews became increasingly difficult to resolve.
03
Building and tracking each page required too much manual work.

A landing page should match the user's intent.

Instead of treating every visitor the same, I mapped four landing-page formats to different levels of awareness and purchase intent.

What I changed

01
Align the brief
Create one structured input shared across paid, creative, design, and engineering.
02
Generate and refine
Use AI to create an initial direction, then focus human review on the decisions that matter.
03
Build in-house
Move production closer to the team instead of relying on an external page builder.
04
Standardize tracking
Define the tracking structure before the page goes live.

Outcome

The first version of the workflow is live in an A/B test, with the teams working from the same brief.

The next step is to evaluate the test and determine which parts of the framework should scale.

What I learned

The biggest challenge was not designing the interface. It was creating a structure that different teams could actually share.

The important shift was making the audience and intent the starting point, rather than asking teams to work from abstract marketing concepts.

That made the workflow easier to understand, easier to build, and easier to reuse.
