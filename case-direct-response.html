Project Overview

Landing pages were being briefed by marketing and paid separately from how they were designed, so the ad angle and the page content routinely fell out of sync. A user would click an ad about one product benefit and land on a page built around a different one, and the resulting conversion issues were hard to diagnose because no one could tell if the problem was the ad, the page, or the mismatch between them.

The goal was to remove that mismatch at the source, by giving paid and design a shared, structured brief, and to cut the time it took to go from that brief to a page in production.

Reflection

The hardest part of this project wasn't the interface, it was getting paid, data, and engineering to agree on a shared structure before any page got built. Framing the audience segment as the input, instead of asking paid to guess at abstract concepts like awareness level, was the shift that made the tool usable by people outside design.

This framework now shares its component library with Mood's broader design system, so pages built through Touchbase stay on brand without a manual design pass. If the pulse check confirms the lift, the next step is extending it past redirect-only pages into the full storefront flow.
