+45% Completion — Quiz UX Improvements

Audited an 8-step product quiz where an incorrect progress indicator and repeated questions caused 75% of users to drop off by the third step, and corrected fifteen distinct friction points, one decision at a time.

Project Overview

The quiz had 8 questions but told users there were only 5. It also asked overlapping questions on different screens, so anyone paying attention hit a point where they felt they'd already answered and dropped off. Completion sat at 24.63%, and 75% of users had already bounced by question three.

The approach was to remove friction rather than redesign visually, every change was judged by whether it helped someone finish, not by whether it looked different.

Key Insights

01
Miscommunicated Progress
The indicator said 5 steps for an actual 8, setting a false expectation that drove mid-funnel drop-off.
02
Logical Inconsistencies
One question asked users to select "more than 6" options when only 6 were available.
03
Mismatched Affordances
A plus icon on the "Other" option implied adding content, not selecting an answer.
04
Weak Visual Hierarchy
"Next" and "Back" competed visually, and instructional copy carried the same weight as the answers.

The Framework

Fifteen individual fixes, grouped into four categories, none of them a visual redesign on its own.

Progress & Expectations
Step count, progress bar, final CTA wording
Visual Hierarchy
CTA dominance, instructional copy weight
Logic & Content
Redundant questions, answer counts, ordering
Typography & Layout
Type scale, spacing, contrast, mobile density

Process

Four of the fifteen changes, each with a clear reason behind it.

01
Progress Indicator
Before: said 5 of 8 steps
After: corrected count, later removed the number entirely
02
CTA Hierarchy
Before: Next and Back visually equal
After: Next full-width, Back demoted to secondary text
03
Answer Ordering
Before: order unrelated to engagement
After: reordered using data from the winning variant
04
Typography
Before: small question text, tight line-height
After: scaled to 24px, tuned for mobile reading

Status & Next Step

+45% Completion
Completion rose from 24.63% to 35.80%, and overall drop-off fell 15 points. Question 1 drop-off fell 20%, question 2 fell 40%, and question 3's drop-off was eliminated entirely once the redundant question was removed. Downstream, subscription revenue per visitor rose 3.93%.

Reflection

None of these fifteen fixes would look impressive on their own, correcting a step count, moving an icon, reordering a list. Together they moved completion by 45%. The lesson was that friction rarely lives in one big broken thing, it accumulates in small mismatches between what the interface says and what it actually does.

The downstream lift in subscription revenue mattered more than the quiz metric itself, it confirmed the fixes weren't just moving people through faster, they were moving more of the right people through to a purchase decision they were ready to make.
